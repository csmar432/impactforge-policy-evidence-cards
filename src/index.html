01 / SCALEWhat is happening?

Food insecurity is not a fringe student experience.

23%of college students in the study

GAO estimated that 3.8 million college students experienced food insecurity in 2020. The estimate comes from a nationally representative Department of Education student study.

For many students, the shortage changes eating patterns.

2.2Mreported very low food security

Among food-insecure students, 2.2 million reported multiple instances of eating less than they should or skipping meals because they could not afford enough food.

03 / POLICY GAPWhat can help?

Potential eligibility is not reported receipt.

59%did not report receiving SNAP

Among potentially SNAP-eligible food-insecure students, 59% did not report receiving benefits. USDA lists student exemptions such as 20 hours of paid work or work-study.

Source notes

Read the evidence with its limits.

The student figures are from GAO’s analysis of the U.S. Department of Education’s 2020 National Postsecondary Student Aid Study. The federal source says these were the most recent available data for that analysis when the report was published.

  • “Food insecurity” is not the same measure as hunger; USDA treats hunger as a possible consequence, not the survey measure itself.
  • The 59% figure applies only to students who were food insecure and potentially eligible for SNAP; it is not a rate for all college students.
  • This prototype explains evidence. It does not determine eligibility or replace a campus adviser or state benefits office.
