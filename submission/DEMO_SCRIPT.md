# 60-second demo script

**0–08 sec — Problem**

“Policy data is public, but it is rarely easy to read. A student may see a food-insecurity statistic without knowing how large the problem is, what the measure means, or where support fits.”

**08–20 sec — Open the brief**

“This is Policy Evidence Cards. The brief has one topic, three evidence cards, and one takeaway. The page is designed to be read in order on a phone or desktop.”

**20–36 sec — Card 01 and Card 02**

“The first card gives the scale: GAO estimated that 23 percent, or 3.8 million, college students experienced food insecurity in 2020. The second card adds severity: 2.2 million food-insecure students reported very low food security, including repeated reductions in food intake or skipped meals.”

**36–49 sec — Card 03**

“The third card makes the policy gap visible. Among food-insecure students who were potentially eligible for SNAP, 59 percent did not report receiving benefits. The card links to the official USDA student rules instead of pretending eligibility is automatic.”

**49–60 sec — Takeaway**

“The evidence shows a gap between potential eligibility and reported receipt. The next question is how students can check the current rules. Every number has a source, a year, and a limitation, so students can ask a better next question.”

**Optional 10-second interaction**

“The reader can choose the next step: understand the context, inspect the definition, or open the official student SNAP rules. A private self-reported clarity check lets us ask whether the takeaway feels clear without collecting personal data.”
